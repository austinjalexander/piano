# P1.1 Keyboard and pulse

Find your way around the keyboard and play a short phrase with a steady count. No previous piano knowledge or method book is required. Use several short sessions as needed; allow roughly 40–60 minutes total practice, plus a later review. These are planning estimates, not a deadline.

## Objectives

- Find white-key names from neighboring black-key groups in different registers.
- Identify finger numbers 1–5 on either hand.
- Count groups of four at a comfortable steady pulse.
- Play the original two-bar phrase with each hand separately, using the indicated rhythm.

- [Notes](notes.md): keyboard map, finger numbers, counting, and worked examples.

## Independent check

Allow up to 10 minutes at a comfortable pace; speed is not the goal. Set a convenient pulse before starting; no tutor hints, diagrams, or solution file during the check. The phrase prompt below may stay visible. A metronome is allowed but optional; record its use and BPM/beat unit, or state that the pulse was self-counted.

1. Without a labeled keyboard, locate and name C, D, E, F, G, A, and B in one region, then C, F, and G in a different region, using the black-key pattern. All ten must be correct without a hint; restart after practice on another attempt if needed.
2. Identify fingers 1 through 5 on each hand correctly.
3. Count eight steady beats aloud as two groups of four. Then play the phrase below once with RH and once with LH separately. Count a four-beat lead-in before each hand. Each performance must have the correct pitches and durations, eight continuous beats with no restart or extra beat, and no reported discomfort. A comfortable slower pulse is acceptable. If pitch or rhythm breaks down, record the attempt and practice before reassessing.

```text
4 beats per bar; each listed note lasts 1 beat unless marked 2.
RH: C4 D4 E4 G4 | E4 D4 C4(2)
LH: C3 D3 E3 G3 | E3 D3 C3(2)
Play one hand at a time. Do not add the hands together yet.
```

Middle C is called C4 here; C3 is the C one octave below. Use another comfortable octave if the keyboard is short and record the substitution.

Complete required exercises and corrections, pass this check, repeat the same playing task successfully on another day, and write your own dated 2–4 sentence reflection before checking P1.1 in [progress](../../../PROGRESS.md). Label self-reported results honestly. Record the actual time or unknown; the time allowance keeps the check short and is not a tempo standard.
