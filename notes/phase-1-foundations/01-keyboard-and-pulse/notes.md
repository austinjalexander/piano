# Keyboard and pulse notes

## A comfortable starting position

Sit where both hands can reach a few neighboring keys comfortably. Let the shoulders and forearms feel easy; avoid holding the wrists rigidly or stretching for distant keys. Begin with a few light key presses, then release. Stop a task that causes pain. A written description cannot verify posture; a teacher or accessible video can provide more specific feedback.

For now use a plain piano sound, a comfortable volume, and no sustain pedal. Check the actual instrument manual for controls rather than guessing from another model.

## Find notes from the black keys

Black keys repeat in groups of two and three. C is the white key immediately to the left of a group of two black keys. D sits between those two black keys; E is the next white key to the right. F is immediately to the left of a group of three black keys; G and A lie in the gaps of that group; B is immediately to its right.

Moving right through the white keys gives C, D, E, F, G, A, B, then the next C. Pitches generally rise as you move right. The same letter in the next register is an octave away; both Cs have the same name but different pitch heights.

**Example:** find F without counting from the bottom of the instrument. Locate a group of three black keys, then touch the white key immediately to its left. Check by moving through F–G–A–B: the following C sits before a group of two black keys.

Middle C lies near the keyboard's center; this repo calls it C4. C3 is an octave below it. Some devices label octaves differently, so record the convention when using MIDI or app feedback. Exact center depends on the keyboard's range.

## Finger numbers

Both hands use the same numbers: thumb 1, index 2, middle 3, ring 4, little finger 5. The thumbs point toward each other when the hands are in a normal playing position. Numbers do not change when the left hand moves.

**Example:** place RH 1–2–3–4–5 over C4–D4–E4–F4–G4. Place LH 5–4–3–2–1 over C3–D3–E3–F3–G3. Play each separately and release any held tension between tries. This five-note position is a starting exercise, not a permanent hand shape for every piece.

## Pulse and duration

A pulse is a regular beat. Count “1 2 3 4 | 1 2 3 4” with equally spaced counts. A bar groups beats; the vertical mark separates bars. In this lesson each bar has four beats, and a quarter note would last one beat. A two-beat note begins on one count and continues through the next.

**Example:** tap once on counts 1 and 3 of “1 2 3 4.” Keep each sound going for two beats in your imagination rather than tapping on every count. When playing, hold each key through its second beat and release as the next note begins.

A metronome can supply one click per beat. If you use it, choose a pulse you can follow easily, for example quarter note = 60 BPM. That example is adjustable, not a passing speed. First try clapping/counting away from the keys if coordinating both is difficult.

## Original two-bar phrase

Read this as a playing recipe; it is not a staff-reading assessment.

```text
Counts:  1  2  3  4 | 1  2  3  4
RH notes C4 D4 E4 G4| E4 D4 C4 --
RH fingers 1 2 3 5 | 3  2  1  hold

LH notes C3 D3 E3 G3| E3 D3 C3 --
LH fingers 5 4 3 1 | 3  4  5  hold
```

The last C lasts beats 3 and 4. Release it on the next imaginary beat 1. Say the rhythm, locate the notes without a pulse, then combine them slowly. Keep each hand separate. This phrase uses only seven key presses across eight beats.

## Common mistakes and checks

- Counting from the keyboard's extreme left can hide the repeating pattern. Find the nearby black-key group instead.
- Left-hand thumb is still finger 1. Touch and name the fingers before the phrase if numbers feel reversed.
- The final C is one key press held for two beats; do not strike it again on beat 4.
- A gap while searching for a note interrupts the pulse. Practice the short note transition without timing, then return to a slower continuous count.
- Do not chase a tempo while the motion feels stiff. Simplify, pause, and try a comfortable pace.
