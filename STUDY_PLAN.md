# Piano practice plan

Start with [the baseline](assessments/baseline.md), then [keyboard and pulse](notes/phase-1-foundations/01-keyboard-and-pulse/README.md) or the earliest demonstrated gap. Read [the goals](GOALS.md) before personalizing the plan.

## Weekly capacity

Provisional example: **20 minutes on five days per week = 100 minutes**, including review and logging. Confirm or replace this budget with the learner. Missed days do not create a practice debt. Progress determines the next block; calendar weeks do not certify readiness.

## A 20-minute session

1. **2 minutes:** settle at the instrument, check comfortable reach, and recall yesterday's target.
2. **4 minutes:** one keyboard or coordination exercise at an easy pulse.
3. **8 minutes:** one short phrase or difficult passage. Isolate one or two bars, choose fingering, practice slowly, then reconnect the passage to its neighbors.
4. **3 minutes:** rotate fresh reading, listening/echoing, or simple improvisation. Early sessions can use keyboard patterns before staff reading is introduced.
5. **3 minutes:** revisit an older phrase, then record the actual time, result, assistance, and next task in [a log](templates/practice-log-template.md).

For 10 minutes: 1 minute setup, 2 technique, 4 phrase work, 2 reading/listening, 1 log. For a longer session, add variety and a break rather than repeating a tense passage for longer. Stop a task that causes pain; do not treat discomfort as a speed-training target.

Choose a tempo at which the task is controlled. BPM means beats per minute; specify the beat unit. Increase speed only after a stable attempt, and record the actual tempo rather than a target you have not reached. A metronome is a reference; counting aloud or tapping can help establish the pulse first.

## First four practice blocks

Each block can occupy a week or longer. These are suggested scopes within the same budget.

- **Block 1 — orientation and pulse:** baseline, keyboard geography, finger numbers, counting, and the original two-bar phrase in [the first lesson](notes/phase-1-foundations/01-keyboard-and-pulse/README.md). Review on another day. Select a preferred musical direction.
- **Block 2 — notation and short melodies:** introduce treble/bass landmarks and whole, half, quarter notes/rests. Work on a few notes at a time and keep keyboard location connected to notation. Author P1.2/P1.3 exercises before assessment; do not claim staff-reading mastery from letter-name exercises.
- **Block 3 — first coordination:** simple separate-hand phrases, then a repeated left-hand note under a slow right-hand melody. Use tiny sections. Choose one accessible method-book selection once the actual book/edition is readable, or author an original short piece.
- **Block 4 — consolidate:** review earlier skills, record or self-report a continuous short performance, explain one practice improvement in your own words, and select the next gap from [progress](PROGRESS.md). Extend previous blocks if needed.

## Longer learning order

1. [Foundations](notes/phase-1-foundations/README.md): keyboard, pulse, notation, listening.
2. [Reading and coordination](notes/phase-2-reading-and-coordination/README.md): separate hands, simple hands-together work, fresh reading.
3. [Keys and chords](notes/phase-3-keys-and-chords/README.md): scales, triads, simple accompaniment and transposition.
4. [Musical playing](notes/phase-4-musical-playing/README.md): phrasing, balance, pedal, varied repertoire.
5. [Independent playing](notes/phase-5-independent-playing/README.md): learning pieces, playing by ear, improvisation, sharing music.

Reading, listening, and enjoyable repertoire continue throughout. Later interests may change this ordering after prerequisite checks. No exam-grade equivalence or completion date is assigned.

At each transition, check [external courses](EXTERNAL_COURSES.md) and [repertoire](REPERTOIRE.md). A teacher assignment, video, or method-book exercise replaces overlapping work in the budget. Record exact lesson/page/bar positions and avoid running several full beginner courses in parallel.
