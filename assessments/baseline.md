# Piano starting check

Allow about 10–15 minutes, including a short discussion. This is a placement check, not a test to study for. Skip anything unfamiliar; do not repeat until it looks successful. No results are recorded yet.

Record results in a dated file using [the assessment template](../templates/assessment-template.md). State whether evidence is self-reported, audio, video, or a teacher's report. Keep unfamiliar, attempted, assisted, and independently demonstrated distinct.

1. Describe previous musical experience, current instrument, preferred music, and realistic practice time.
2. Find a group of two black keys and a group of three. If you know them, locate C, F, and G in two different keyboard regions.
3. Tap a comfortable steady pulse for eight beats. Count two groups of four aloud, then clap four single-beat sounds and two two-beat sounds.
4. If comfortable, play C–D–E–F–G and back with each hand separately. State the hand, keyboard region, fingering, and whether the pulse continued. Skip if you do not know how.
5. If you read notation, use a previously unpracticed short excerpt from a readable book you own. Record its source, clefs, bars, preparation, and what happened. If no excerpt is available, leave reading unassessed; letter names do not test staff reading.
6. If you already play, perform a familiar short passage and explain one difficult spot. Hands together, chords, pedal, and ear playing are optional probes of existing experience, not entry requirements.

Choose the earliest useful gap after the check. A new player starts with [P1.1](../notes/phase-1-foundations/01-keyboard-and-pulse/README.md). An experienced player can receive a targeted lesson without marking skipped topics complete. Assess those separately if completion is needed.
