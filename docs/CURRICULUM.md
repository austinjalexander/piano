# Piano curriculum

This is a proposed learning sequence, not an examination syllabus. Topic IDs match [PROGRESS.md](../PROGRESS.md). Phase READMEs define scope; only P1.1 currently has a complete lesson and playing check. Author a concrete task and rubric before assessing other topics.

## Phase 1 Foundations

Start without prior piano knowledge. See [phase scope](../notes/phase-1-foundations/README.md).

- **P1.1 Keyboard and pulse:** locate C through B using black-key groups, identify finger numbers, count four beats, and play a short phrase with each hand separately. [Authored lesson](../notes/phase-1-foundations/01-keyboard-and-pulse/README.md).
- **P1.2 Rhythm and rests:** clap and play whole, half, and quarter notes/rests in simple meter; introduce paired eighth notes after the pulse is reliable. Requires a basic pulse from P1.1.
- **P1.3 Grand staff landmarks:** connect treble/bass clefs, middle C, neighboring notes, and keyboard position. Requires keyboard familiarity; combine with P1.2 for reading.
- **P1.4 Listening and echoing:** distinguish higher/lower and same/different; echo a short rhythm and a small melodic contour. Begin alongside P1.1.

## Phase 2 Reading and coordination

See [phase scope](../notes/phase-2-reading-and-coordination/README.md). Build on P1.1–P1.3; carry P1.4 alongside playing.

- **P2.1 Separate-hand reading:** play short notated phrases with planned fingering and consistent rhythm.
- **P2.2 Hands together:** combine a simple melody with a sparse bass part without losing the pulse. Start with P2.1 and simpler textures than separate-hand work.
- **P2.3 Fresh reading:** scan clefs, meter, starting notes, and patterns, then play a new easier passage continuously. Assess fresh material separately from practiced repertoire.

## Phase 3 Keys and chords

See [phase scope](../notes/phase-3-keys-and-chords/README.md). Requires comfortable keyboard geography and basic separate-hand playing; introduce theory in short connected examples.

- **P3.1 Scales and keys:** construct a major scale from whole/half steps; learn a suitable one-octave fingering, then selected minor patterns. Begin with C, add G/F when ready. No universal speed target.
- **P3.2 Triads and inversions:** build major/minor triads, hear their character, and move between useful inversions.
- **P3.3 Accompaniment and transposition:** use simple I–IV–V–I progressions and transpose a short melody to a familiar key. Requires P3.1/P3.2 and simple coordination from P2.2.

## Phase 4 Musical playing

See [phase scope](../notes/phase-4-musical-playing/README.md). Apply to repertoire that fits the learner's current reading and coordination.

- **P4.1 Phrasing and articulation:** shape a short phrase using dynamics, legato, and staccato with an audible intention.
- **P4.2 Balance and pedal:** bring out a melody over a quieter accompaniment; add pedal changes that preserve clear harmony. Pedal requires a suitable instrument and stable unpedaled playing.
- **P4.3 Repertoire and retention:** prepare contrasting short pieces, recover from mistakes, and revisit them over time. Define criteria for each actual arrangement, not just a title.

## Phase 5 Independent playing

See [phase scope](../notes/phase-5-independent-playing/README.md). Small ear and creative tasks can begin earlier; independent outcomes draw on the relevant prior skills.

- **P5.1 Learn a new piece:** choose an accessible arrangement, map sections, diagnose passages, and make a practice plan.
- **P5.2 Ear and creativity:** recover a short melody by ear and vary an original phrase over a familiar chord pattern. Requires basic listening and, for chords, P3.2/P3.3.
- **P5.3 Share and maintain:** prepare a short set, make a recording or play for a listener, reflect, and maintain chosen pieces. Sharing remains optional if the learner prefers private playing; adapt the evidence task accordingly.
