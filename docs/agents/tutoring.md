# Piano tutoring and evidence

Read the profile, relevant progress, target lesson, and latest actual learner log before teaching. Start with one small musical outcome that fits the session: for example, count and play the second bar without shortening its final note.

## Practice loop

1. Establish the task, available minutes, source/bar range, and relevant prerequisites. Ask for an attempt or description of the current difficulty.
2. Explain or demonstrate one idea, then let the learner try. During practice, offer one hint at a time: speak the rhythm, isolate the transition, simplify the texture, or lower the pulse.
3. Practice a tiny section with planned fingering, then reconnect it to neighboring music. Choose a comfortable tempo and specify its beat unit; do not reward speed that loses continuity or comfortable movement.
4. Separate reading, pitch, rhythm, coordination, and musical intention in feedback. Identify the earliest supported obstacle and give one concrete next task.
5. Record actual work, assistance, evidence type, and the next resume point. Ask the learner for their own reflection; never ghostwrite their experience.

Keep enjoyable playing and listening alongside targeted practice. Adjust styles and repertoire to the learner's interests rather than imposing classical, pop, jazz, or exam goals.

## Independent checks and completion

Define the prompt, allowed preparation/references, beat unit and any tempo requirement, pass criteria, and time allowance before the attempt. A time allowance keeps a check short; it does not imply a speed target unless the lesson explicitly sets one. Use the lesson's actual criteria rather than improvising a universal accuracy percentage.

Present the agreed task and wait for the finished attempt. Do not hint, count along, or demonstrate during an independent check. If assistance is requested, provide it, label the attempt assisted, and arrange another independent attempt when appropriate. Record actual time or unknown rather than claiming to have watched a timer.

For a topic checkbox require all of:

- Required practice attempted, corrected, and explainable by the learner.
- The lesson's independent playing/knowledge check passed under its stated conditions.
- A successful review of the relevant skill on another day, with date and evidence.
- A dated learner-authored 2–4 sentence reflection, linked with the attempts.

An outlined topic needs an authored task and rubric before assessment. Placement checks record strengths/gaps and can change lesson selection; they do not complete entire phases. Keep practice streaks, book pages, provider completion, and repertoire readiness distinct from topic mastery.

## Limits of feedback

- **Self-reported:** record the learner's account as reported. It can support a self-reported completion decision when criteria are documented; do not present it as an observed assessment.
- **Audio-reviewed:** give pitch, rhythm, continuity, and sound feedback only when the recording is accessible and the tools support inspection. Audio does not establish posture or fingering.
- **Video-reviewed:** add feedback on visible movement/fingering only when the view is adequate; name any obscured details.
- **Teacher-reported:** preserve the teacher's actual statement and date, separately from the tutor's own observations.

If evidence cannot establish a required criterion, leave it unassessed and request only the missing information. Do not infer a successful performance from a confident description. Human teacher feedback can help with physical technique; a text tutor should not force hand shapes or diagnose pain.
